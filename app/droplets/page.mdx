> As someone who has spent thousands of dollars on plugins over the years, let me give you a few tips:
> 
> 1) Pro Tools is the only DAW with a propriety plugin format (AAX). With the exception of those with crappy VST support (Audacity and the like) all plugins should work in all DAWs
>
> 2) Don't use Waves. It's a predatory company that makes you pay for your plugins multiple times.
>
> 3) Free plugins, and very good free plugins are available in abundance (I'll grab BPB's list in a moment: https://bedroomproducersblog.com/free-vst-plugins/ )--don't buy stuff unless it fills a very specific need that freeware can't, like footsteps, masking, surround sound support, etc. Don't you dare pay for a software compressor
> 
> 4) A lot of plugins are going to sell themselves on being hardware emulations and try to charge a lot of money--but unless that company is also the device manufacturer or it's an incredible niche device, someone else has probably also made that emulator for cheaper
>
> 5) Plugins excel when you automate parameters. Open up some automation lanes and change stuff slowly for maximum punch. It's definitely a learning curve but one that you'll appreciate once you're changing reverb with your scene change
> Bedroom Producers Blog
